What this system is, conceptually

Reading a tumor,
the way a machine sees it.

OncoSeg is a deep-learning pipeline that takes a patient's 3D brain-MRI, outlines the tumor voxel-by-voxel, flags where it is unsure, and then turns those outlines into the standard clinical verdict oncologists use to judge whether a treatment is working — CR / PR / SD / PD under RECIST 1.1.

The clinical problem

Why automate this at all?

In oncology trials, judging whether a drug works means measuring tumors on scans over time. Done by hand it is slow (15–30 min/scan), subjective (radiologists disagree 20–40% on the boundary), and historically reduced to a single 2D diameter. OncoSeg's premise: do the measurement in full 3D, reproducibly, and carry an explicit confidence signal alongside it.

The architecture, in plain terms

A hybrid: global eyes, local hands

Two ideas are fused. A Swin Transformer encoder looks at the whole volume and reasons about long-range context ("is this blob part of the same tumor as that one?"). A CNN decoder rebuilds crisp, local voxel boundaries. Between them, cross-attention skip connections let the decoder ask the encoder for exactly the features it needs — instead of blindly concatenating them.

The five moving parts

  • Swin encoder — hierarchical windowed self-attention over the 3D volume; 4 stages at ¼→1/32 resolution, channels 48→96→192→384.
  • Cross-attention skips — decoder features are queries; encoder features are keys/values. The decoder selectively pulls relevant context across the U.
  • CNN decoder — transposed-conv upsampling restores full resolution and sharp edges.
  • Deep supervision — auxiliary heads at each decoder scale add loss signal, stabilizing training and helping small lesions.
  • MC-Dropout — dropout left on at inference; multiple passes give a per-voxel uncertainty/entropy map.
  • Temporal attention (optional) — fuses baseline vs follow-up bottlenecks to capture change directly.

Bottom line

Strong engineering scaffold, unreliable scientific claims.

Code quality, CI, Docker/API, and DICOM I/O are genuinely good. But the two flagship results — "OncoSeg significantly outperforms UNet3D" and "the model is well-calibrated" — are not supported by the committed data, and on close inspection point the opposite way. Highest-leverage fixes: remove/correct the Wilcoxon claim, report foreground-only ECE, fix the TC/WT/ET label permutation, repair the RECIST longest-diameter bug, and reconcile the duplicate-number documentation.

Findings are reproducible from the repository's committed artifacts (per-subject Dice .npy, eval JSON) and source at the cited file:line anchors.
